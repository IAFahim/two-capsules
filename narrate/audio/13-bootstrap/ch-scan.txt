The scan collects every type implementing the bootstrap interface, skips the abstract ones, and then does something worth knowing about. If one candidate is assignable from another, the more derived one wins silently. That is the whole inheritance trick: a package ships a bootstrap, you subclass it, and yours quietly replaces it with no registration step anywhere. But if two candidates are unrelated, the engine logs an error and picks arbitrarily. So the mechanism that makes layering effortless is also the one that makes a second unrelated bootstrap a genuinely confusing bug.
