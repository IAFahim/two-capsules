When the buffer is drained, the sender opens a packet on the reliable pipeline, writes a five byte header, and then fills the rest with as many whole messages as will fit. Never a partial one. If a single message will not fit in one packet at all, that is a hard error and it throws, because there is no fragmentation on this path. So a large remote call is not slow, it is impossible, and the failure is loud rather than silent. That is the right choice, and it is the opposite of how the snapshot path handles the same problem.
