Once it knows, it needs somewhere both worlds can see. That is the shared static: a global that compiled and interpreted code agree points at the same bytes. The managed side marshals a delegate into a raw function pointer and parks it there. The native side loads it and calls it. In the other direction a managed delegate is compiled to native and invoked. Every crossing is an indirect call over a marshalling boundary, and everything on the far side runs at managed speed. That is the real reason a stray managed call in a hot loop costs what it costs.
